# Economy & Policy

## Macroeconomic Landscape

The U.S. macroeconomic backdrop at the end of March 2026 is defined by cooler inflation, still-restrictive monetary policy, a labor market that is softening but not contracting sharply, and persistent pressure on household budgets from housing, insurance, and debt costs. Even though the pace of inflation has slowed substantially from its 2022 peak, the cumulative increase in prices over the last three years still means many households have less purchasing power than before the inflation surge [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm) [*(BLS, 2023)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_01122023.htm).

### Inflation and purchasing power

Inflation has cooled, but the mix matters more than the headline. Goods inflation has largely normalized, while shelter and labor-intensive services continue to keep many monthly bills elevated.

- **CPI rose 2.8% year over year** in February 2026 [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)
- **Core CPI rose 3.1% year over year** in February 2026 [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)
- **PCE prices rose 2.5%, and core PCE rose 2.6%** over the 12 months ending January 2026 [*(BEA, 2026)*](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-january-2026)
- **Shelter prices rose 4.3%** over the 12 months ending February 2026 [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.t02.htm)
- **Food prices rose 2.1%**, while **energy prices fell 0.2%** over the same period [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.t02.htm)
- **Services less energy services rose 4.1%** in February 2026, showing continued stickiness in labor-heavy categories [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.t02.htm)
- **Real average hourly earnings increased 1.2%** over the 12 months ending February 2026, which means wage gains are again outpacing inflation, but only modestly [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/realer.nr0.htm)
- **CPI was 2.9% in December 2024**, down sharply from **6.5% in December 2022**, showing the broader disinflation trend [*(BLS, 2025)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_01152025.htm) [*(BLS, 2023)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_01122023.htm)

For households, the practical issue is that inflation is now concentrated in recurring essentials rather than discretionary goods. Rent, owners' equivalent rent, insurance, medical services, and interest-sensitive expenses can keep budgets tight even when headline inflation looks close to normal.

### Federal Reserve policy and rate outlook

The Federal Reserve is still holding policy in restrictive territory, although it has already moved down from the 2024 peak. The March 2026 meeting reinforced that officials want more evidence before signaling a faster easing cycle.

- **The federal funds target range is 4.25%-4.50%** after the March 19, 2026 FOMC meeting [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)
- **The Fed held rates steady in March** and said uncertainty around the economic outlook has increased [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)
- **The Fed will slow Treasury runoff to $5 billion per month starting in April 2026**, down from $25 billion, while keeping the **agency MBS cap at $35 billion** [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)
- **The median FOMC projection for the fed funds rate was 3.9% at year-end 2026** in the December 2025 Summary of Economic Projections [*(Federal Reserve, 2025)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20251210.htm)
- **The target range was 5.25%-5.50% through much of 2024**, highlighting how much tightening remains in the system even after late-2025 cuts [*(Federal Reserve, 2024)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20240731a.htm)
- **Federal funds futures implied expectations for additional rate cuts by year-end 2026** as of late March 2026, though those expectations remain data-dependent and can shift quickly [*(CME Group, 2026)*](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool.html)

The likely six to 12 month path is gradual easing if inflation continues to cool and labor-market slack increases modestly. The main reason that path could be delayed is sticky services inflation, especially in shelter and other non-tradable categories.

### Labor market and AI-related disruption signals

The labor market is easing from very tight conditions, but it does not yet resemble a recessionary collapse. Hiring has slowed, job openings have come down from 2022 highs, and some more cyclical categories are weakening first.

- **The unemployment rate was 4.1%** in February 2026 [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)
- **Nonfarm payrolls increased by 151,000** in February 2026 [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)
- **Health care added 52,000 jobs**, **government added 21,000**, and **social assistance added 11,000** in February 2026 [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)
- **Temporary help services lost 12,300 jobs** in February 2026, a category often watched as an early warning sign for softer labor demand [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)
- **Average hourly earnings rose 4.0% year over year** in February 2026 [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)
- **Job openings were 7.7 million** on the last business day of January 2026, down from pandemic-era highs above 12 million in 2022 [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.nr0.htm) [*(BLS, 2022)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/jolts_10042022.htm)
- **The quits rate was 2.1%** in January 2026, below the more worker-confident levels seen in 2022 [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.nr0.htm)
- **41% of employers expect to reduce workforces where AI can automate tasks by 2030** [*(WEF, 2025)*](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)
- **Workers in more AI-exposed occupations report greater task change and training needs**, but that evidence points more to job redesign than to economy-wide displacement so far [*(OECD, 2025)*](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/06/ai-jobs-and-skills_4e2d9e4b.html)

The strongest current AI-related signal is slower hiring for routine support and administrative work, not a broad wave of layoffs directly attributed to AI. Over the next year, the more likely household risk is reduced hiring velocity and higher skill requirements, rather than immediate mass unemployment.

### Housing and affordability

Housing remains one of the clearest channels through which households feel macroeconomic strain. Mortgage rates are below some recent peaks, but affordability is still poor because prices remain high and supply turnover is constrained by the lock-in effect from older low-rate mortgages.

- **The 30-year fixed mortgage rate averaged 6.67%** for the week ending March 19, 2026 [*(Freddie Mac, 2026)*](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)
- **That rate was 6.74% in March 2025 and 6.87% in March 2024**, showing only limited relief over the last two years [*(Freddie Mac, 2026)*](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms/archive)
- **The S&P CoreLogic Case-Shiller U.S. National Home Price Index rose 4.1% year over year** in December 2025 [*(S&P Dow Jones Indices, 2026)*](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/indexnews/announcements/20260224-1471000/1471000_cshomeprice-release-02242026.pdf)
- **Median asking rent was $1,694** in February 2026, up **0.4%** from a year earlier [*(Realtor.com, 2026)*](https://www.realtor.com/research/february-2026-rent/)
- **Shelter inflation was 4.3%** in the CPI basket in February 2026, reinforcing how housing costs continue to outpace headline inflation [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.t02.htm)
- **Existing-home sales were running at a 4.26 million annual rate** in February 2026, still historically subdued by post-pandemic standards [*(NAR, 2026)*](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/existing-home-sales-climbed-4-2-in-february)

If the Fed cuts gradually over the next six to 12 months, mortgage rates could ease somewhat, but affordability is unlikely to improve quickly unless rates fall more meaningfully or income growth outpaces home-price growth for a sustained period.

### Equity markets, concentration, and valuation risk

U.S. equity indexes remain strong, supported in large part by AI-related optimism and resilient earnings expectations. For households with retirement accounts, the key issue is that headline index performance still masks narrow leadership and elevated concentration in a small group of mega-cap firms. These observations are not financial advice but provide a framework for discussion.

- **The S&P 500 closed at 5,776.65** on March 24, 2026 [*(S&P Dow Jones Indices, 2026)*](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/indices/equity/sp-500/)
- **The Nasdaq Composite closed at 18,182.62** on March 24, 2026 [*(Nasdaq, 2026)*](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/index/comp/historical)
- **The top 10 companies represented 37.3% of S&P 500 market capitalization** at year-end 2025 [*(S&P Dow Jones Indices, 2026)*](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/indexnews/announcements/20260112-1485232/1485232_sp500top10concentrationye2025.pdf)
- **The S&P 500 forward P/E ratio was 21.4** in March 2026, above the **5-year average of 19.9** and **10-year average of 18.3** [*(Yardeni Research, 2026)*](https://www.yardeni.com/pub/peacockfeval.pdf)
- **The Federal Reserve's November 2025 Financial Stability Report identified elevated asset valuations as a notable vulnerability** [*(Federal Reserve, 2025)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-202511.pdf)

The main opportunity is that AI investment may continue to support productivity and earnings. The main risk is that portfolios tied heavily to market-cap weighted indexes may be more exposed to a narrow set of AI and tech names than investors realize.

### Consumer spending, savings, and credit stress

Consumers are still spending, but the balance sheet picture is less comfortable than it was earlier in the cycle. Savings rates remain modest, and credit stress has risen, especially among lower-income and more debt-burdened households.

- **Real personal consumption expenditures rose 0.3%** in January 2026 [*(BEA, 2026)*](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-january-2026)
- **The personal saving rate was 4.1%** in January 2026 [*(BEA, 2026)*](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-january-2026)
- **Credit card serious delinquency reached 7.18%** in Q4 2025 [*(Federal Reserve Bank of New York, 2026)*](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260213)
- **Total household debt rose to $18.04 trillion** in Q4 2025 [*(Federal Reserve Bank of New York, 2026)*](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260213)
- **Consumer sentiment was 73.6** in the University of Michigan's March 2026 reading [*(University of Michigan, 2026)*](https://data.sca.isr.umich.edu/)
- **The Conference Board Consumer Confidence Index was 101.3** in February 2026 [*(The Conference Board, 2026)*](https://www.conference-board.org/topics/consumer-confidence)

This mix suggests that households are still supporting growth, but with thinner buffers. If job growth slows further or rates stay high for longer, delinquency pressure could broaden beyond the most stretched borrowers.

### Recession indicators and growth trajectory

Recession signals remain mixed rather than uniformly negative. The most likely base case is slower expansion, not a confirmed downturn, but several indicators still argue for caution over the next six to 12 months.

- **Real GDP grew at a 2.3% annual rate** in Q4 2025 [*(BEA, 2026)*](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gross-domestic-product-fourth-quarter-and-year-2025-second-estimate)
- **Real GDP grew 2.8% in 2024** and **2.9% in 2023**, showing a gradual cooling trend rather than a sudden stop [*(BEA, 2025)*](https://www.bea.gov/news/2025/gross-domestic-product-fourth-quarter-and-year-2024-advance-estimate) [*(BEA, 2024)*](https://www.bea.gov/news/2024/gross-domestic-product-fourth-quarter-and-year-2023-second-estimate)
- **The 10-year minus 2-year Treasury spread was 0.18 percentage point** on March 24, 2026, indicating the curve has moved out of deep inversion [*(FRED, 2026)*](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/T10Y2Y)
- **The Conference Board Leading Economic Index fell 0.2% in January 2026** and was **down 1.0% over the prior six months** [*(The Conference Board, 2026)*](https://www.conference-board.org/topics/us-leading-indicators)
- **The OECD projected global growth of 3.1% in 2026**, while warning that trade fragmentation and geopolitical tensions remain downside risks [*(OECD, 2025)*](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-outlook-volume-2025-issue-2_4a60f2f4-en.html)

For individuals, the practical takeaway is that the economy still looks more like a late-cycle slowdown than a recession. That said, labor-market cooling, elevated debt-service burdens, expensive housing, and concentrated equity valuations leave households more vulnerable to shocks than headline GDP alone suggests.

## Geopolitical & Market Risks

Geopolitical and market risks at the end of March 2026 are less about one obvious crisis than about several pressure points moving at once. Oil prices are still moderate, the yen carry trade is still open, and global growth has not broken, but sanctions, tariffs, shipping disruptions, and leverage in non-bank finance could still transmit quickly into consumer prices, retirement accounts, and borrowing costs. For readers, the practical takeaway is that the next shock may begin abroad, then show up at home through gasoline, groceries, insurance, freight costs, and market volatility.

- **Brent spot crude closed at $73.63 per barrel, and WTI at $69.16 on March 24, 2026** [*(U.S. EIA, 2026)*](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/hist/LeafHandler.ashx?n=PET&s=DCOILBRENTEU&f=D)
- **The Federal Reserve held the federal funds target range at 4.25%-4.50% on March 19, 2026** [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)
- **The Bank of Japan kept its uncollateralized overnight call rate target at around 0.50% in January 2025**, the latest rate-setting step cited in the current policy path [*(Bank of Japan, 2025)*](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2025/k250124a.pdf)
- **Non-bank financial intermediation vulnerabilities remain significant**, according to the FSB's 2025 annual report [*(FSB, 2025)*](https://www.fsb.org/2025/11/fsb-annual-report-2025/)

The broad message is caution, not prediction. These observations are not financial advice but provide a framework for discussion.

### Oil and energy

Oil prices remain below the extremes seen after Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, but the market is still being actively managed by OPEC+ and remains exposed to chokepoints and sanctions enforcement. That matters because energy shocks often hit households indirectly, through freight, food distribution, utilities, and airline costs, before they show up as a dramatic jump at the gas pump.

- **Brent was $73.63, and WTI was $69.16 on March 24, 2026** [*(U.S. EIA, 2026)*](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/hist/LeafHandler.ashx?n=PET&s=DCOILBRENTEU&f=D)
- **The EIA projected Brent would average $74 per barrel in 2026** in its March 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook [*(U.S. EIA, 2026)*](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/)
- **OPEC+ extended production adjustments through the end of 2026** [*(OPEC, 2024)*](https://www.opec.org/opec_web/en/press_room/7283.htm)
- **The IEA said in March 2026 that non-OPEC+ supply growth is exceeding demand growth**, helping keep the market broadly supplied for now [*(IEA, 2026)*](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-march-2026)
- **Red Sea attacks raised freight costs and delivery times**, with inflation effects arriving later rather than immediately [*(IMF, 2024)*](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/03/28/red-sea-attacks-disrupt-global-trade)
- **U.S. consumer energy prices rose 0.2% year over year in February 2026**, while electricity rose 2.5% and utility gas service fell 6.0% [*(BLS, 2026)*](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)

The key hidden risk is not only crude itself. A disruption in the Strait of Hormuz, the Red Sea, or Black Sea export routes could lift shipping insurance, diesel-linked transport costs, and imported goods prices even if benchmark crude moves only gradually.

### Yen carry trade and BOJ normalization

The yen carry trade remains one of the most important cross-market fragilities because it links Japanese funding costs to global appetite for equities, credit, and emerging-market assets. The mid-2024 unwind showed how quickly a stronger yen and changing BOJ expectations can force deleveraging across crowded trades.

- **The BOJ ended negative interest rates and yield curve control in March 2024** [*(Bank of Japan, 2024)*](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/about/press/koen_2024/ko240319a.htm)
- **The BOJ raised its policy rate to around 0.25% in July 2024** [*(Bank of Japan, 2024)*](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/announcements/release_2024/k240731a.pdf)
- **The BOJ raised the policy rate further to around 0.50% in January 2025** [*(Bank of Japan, 2025)*](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2025/k250124a.pdf)
- **The Fed remains at 4.25%-4.50% as of March 19, 2026**, leaving a sizable but narrower rate gap than in 2024 [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)
- **The BIS warned that leveraged trades can amplify volatility when funding conditions shift abruptly** [*(BIS, 2024)*](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2024e.htm)

If U.S. rates drift lower while Japan continues gradual normalization, yen-funded trades become less attractive and more unstable. In that scenario, the first pressure points would likely be highly valued technology shares, lower-quality credit, and liquid emerging-market assets that investors can sell quickly.

### Trade wars and tariffs

Tariffs are no longer just a political headline. Many of the major U.S. and EU measures were finalized in 2024, but their effects continue into 2026 through phased implementation, supplier shifts, and higher input costs.

- **The U.S. finalized Section 301 tariff increases in September 2024** [*(USTR, 2024)*](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2024/september/final-modifications-section-301-actions-chinas-targeting-maritime-logistics-and-shipbuilding-sectors)
- **U.S. tariffs include 100% on Chinese electric vehicles** [*(USTR, 2024)*](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2024/september/final-modifications-section-301-actions-chinas-targeting-maritime-logistics-and-shipbuilding-sectors)
- **U.S. tariffs include 50% on semiconductors** [*(USTR, 2024)*](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2024/september/final-modifications-section-301-actions-chinas-targeting-maritime-logistics-and-shipbuilding-sectors)
- **U.S. tariffs include 25% on steel, aluminum, and several battery-related categories** [*(USTR, 2024)*](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2024/september/final-modifications-section-301-actions-chinas-targeting-maritime-logistics-and-shipbuilding-sectors)
- **The European Commission imposed definitive countervailing duties on battery electric vehicle imports from China in October 2024** [*(European Commission, 2024)*](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_24_5589)
- **The USITC reported that tariff pass-through can raise domestic prices and alter sourcing patterns**, depending on competition and supply elasticity [*(USITC, 2024)*](https://www.usitc.gov/research_and_analysis/trade_shifts_2024_special_topic.htm)

The inflation effect is often indirect and delayed. Companies can absorb some tariff costs at first, but over time they tend to pass them through via higher final prices, supplier diversification costs, and larger inventory buffers.

### Sanctions and financial fragmentation

Sanctions on Russia, export controls on China, and the search for payment-system redundancy are pushing the global economy toward more fragmentation. This does not mean the dollar is about to lose its central role, but it does mean more trade is being routed through parallel channels that are less transparent and potentially harder to stabilize in a crisis.

- **The U.S. Treasury continues broad Russia-related sanctions covering banks, energy, military supply chains, and evasion networks** [*(U.S. Treasury, 2026)*](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financial-sanctions/sanctions-programs-and-country-information/russia-related-sanctions)
- **The EU sanctions timeline shows repeated expansion of restrictions on Russian banking, transport, energy, and trade** [*(Council of the EU, 2026)*](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/sanctions-against-russia/timeline-sanctions-against-russia/)
- **BRICS leaders backed broader use of local currencies and cross-border payment infrastructure in the 2024 Kazan Declaration** [*(BRICS, 2024)*](https://www.mid.ru/en/foreign_policy/news/1975362/)
- **China's Cross-Border Interbank Payment System continues to expand renminbi clearing participation and capacity** [*(CIPS, 2026)*](https://www.cips.com.cn/en/index/index.html)
- **The Federal Reserve maintains standing U.S. dollar liquidity swap lines with major central banks**, a reminder that dollar backstops still matter most in a crisis [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/central-bank-liquidity-swaps.htm)

The practical implication is mixed. Fragmentation can reduce efficiency and visibility, but it can also create backup channels for countries trying to reduce sanctions risk or payment-system dependence.

### Emerging-market stress

Emerging markets are not facing a single synchronized crisis, but many still carry the same vulnerabilities that mattered in earlier stress episodes, especially high external debt, weak reserve buffers, and sensitivity to dollar funding costs. This matters to U.S. readers because EM stress can feed back into commodity prices, global credit conditions, and risk appetite.

- **The IMF said in its January 2026 World Economic Outlook Update that global growth remains subdued and uncertainty elevated** [*(IMF, 2026)*](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WEO/Issues/2026/01/14/world-economic-outlook-update-january-2026)
- **The World Bank said developing economies have faced record debt-service burdens in recent years** [*(World Bank, 2026)*](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/debt-statistics/idr)
- **The BIS noted that foreign-exchange leverage and dollar funding stress can amplify global volatility** [*(BIS, 2024)*](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2409.htm)
- **The Fed's standing dollar swap lines remain an emergency backstop for global funding stress** [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/central-bank-liquidity-swaps.htm)

A strong dollar or wider credit spreads can turn a manageable refinancing problem into a crisis quickly. Countries with large current-account deficits or heavy short-term external borrowing remain the most exposed.

### Black swan indicators to watch

The most useful warning signs are often outside mainstream recession coverage. Shipping chokepoints, funding-market stress, non-bank leverage, and extreme market concentration can all signal trouble before labor-market or GDP data do.

- **Red Sea disruption remains inflation-relevant because rerouting raises freight costs and delivery times** [*(IMF, 2024)*](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/03/28/red-sea-attacks-disrupt-global-trade)
- **Non-bank financial vulnerabilities remain significant**, according to the FSB's 2025 annual report [*(FSB, 2025)*](https://www.fsb.org/2025/11/fsb-annual-report-2025/)
- **The Federal Reserve's November 2025 Financial Stability Report highlighted elevated asset valuations and the risk of sharp repricing** [*(Federal Reserve, 2025)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-202511.pdf)
- **The top 10 companies accounted for 37.3% of S&P 500 market capitalization at year-end 2025** [*(S&P Dow Jones Indices, 2026)*](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/indexnews/announcements/20260112-1485232/1485232_sp500top10concentrationye2025.pdf)
- **The Fed continues to maintain central bank dollar liquidity swap lines as a market-stress backstop** [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/central-bank-liquidity-swaps.htm)

The main takeaway is that the next destabilizing event may not begin with a domestic recession signal. It could begin with a tanker incident, a sanctions escalation, a yen-funded deleveraging, or a liquidity shock in non-bank finance, then spread into everyday prices and household balance sheets.

## Policy & Regulation

Policy is a direct market driver in March 2026, especially for AI deployment, digital-asset infrastructure, trade costs, Treasury financing, and the path of monetary conditions. The biggest changes since earlier versions of this section are that the White House has now published the AI Action Plan required by **Executive Order 14179**, major U.S. crypto bills still remain pending rather than enacted, the debt limit remains a live fiscal constraint, and the Federal Reserve has slowed Treasury runoff while holding rates restrictive.

- **Executive Order 14179 is the operative federal AI order** after **Executive Order 14110** was revoked; EO 14179 was signed on January 23, 2025, and published in the *Federal Register* on January 31, 2025 [*(Federal Register, 2025)*](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/31/2025-02095/removing-barriers-to-american-leadership-in-artificial-intelligence)
- **The White House AI Action Plan has now been released**, fulfilling EO 14179's directive to produce a federal plan for AI leadership and deployment [*(White House, 2025)*](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf)
- **No comprehensive federal AI statute has been enacted** as of March 31, 2026, based on Congress.gov legislative records [*(Congress.gov, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.congress.gov/search?q=%7B%22source%22%3A%22legislation%22%2C%22search%22%3A%22artificial%20intelligence%22%7D)
- **The GENIUS Act, S. 394, and the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act of 2025, H.R. 3633, remain pending bills**, not public laws [*(Congress.gov, S.394)*](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/394), [*(Congress.gov, H.R.3633)*](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/3633)
- **The FOMC kept the federal funds target range at 4.25%-4.50%** on March 19, 2026, and **reduced the monthly Treasury redemption cap from $25 billion to $5 billion starting in April 2026** [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)
- **The statutory debt limit was reinstated on January 2, 2025**, after the suspension in the **Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023, H.R. 3746, Public Law 118-5** expired [*(U.S. Treasury, 2026)*](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financial-markets-financial-institutions-and-fiscal-service/debt-limit), [*(Congress.gov, 2023)*](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-bill/3746)

The practical takeaway is that policy is advancing through executive action, agency guidance, phased compliance, and cross-border divergence more than through one clean federal legislative package. For households and businesses, that means planning risk now comes from implementation details as much as from headline votes.

### AI policy

U.S. AI policy has shifted from the 2023 safety-focused framework to a 2025-2026 competitiveness, procurement, and deployment framework, but Congress still has not enacted an economy-wide AI law. The important update is that the federal government has moved beyond EO 14179 alone and now has an official AI Action Plan plus OMB operating rules for agencies.

- **Executive Order 14179, "Removing Barriers to American Leadership in Artificial Intelligence,"** was signed on January 23, 2025, and directed the development of an AI Action Plan within 180 days [*(Federal Register, 2025)*](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/31/2025-02095/removing-barriers-to-american-leadership-in-artificial-intelligence)
- **The AI Action Plan was published in July 2025**, setting federal priorities around infrastructure, innovation, procurement, talent, and international competitiveness [*(White House, 2025)*](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf)
- **OMB Memorandum M-25-21** set requirements for federal agency use of AI, including governance, risk management, and public-trust safeguards [*(OMB, 2025)*](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf)
- **OMB Memorandum M-25-22** established minimum standards for federal procurement of AI systems and services [*(OMB, 2025)*](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/M-25-22-Driving-Efficient-Acquisition-of-Artificial-Intelligence-in-Government.pdf)
- **NIST's Generative AI Profile, NIST AI 600-1**, provides implementation guidance tied to the AI Risk Management Framework [*(NIST, 2024)*](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
- **NIST's Adversarial Machine Learning taxonomy, NIST AI 100-2e2025**, adds technical definitions for model security and attack surfaces [*(NIST, 2025)*](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-2e2025.pdf)
- **No major federal AI law, including any measure described as "OBBBA," appears as enacted federal law** in Congress.gov records as of March 31, 2026 [*(Congress.gov, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.congress.gov/)
- **The CHIPS and Science Act, H.R. 4346, Public Law 117-167**, remains the main enacted federal industrial-policy statute supporting semiconductor capacity and AI-related compute supply chains [*(Congress.gov, 2022)*](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/4346)

The gap is still a binding federal AI statute covering liability, transparency, labor impacts, frontier model obligations, and federal preemption of state rules. Until that changes, the U.S. system remains a patchwork of executive orders, procurement standards, technical guidance, sector regulators, and state law.

### Digital asset regulation

Digital-asset regulation remains active, but the key congressional market-structure and stablecoin bills are still pending. That means the real policy environment is still being shaped by executive action, SEC and CFTC enforcement, ETF approvals, bank and payments supervision, and foreign rulebooks.

- **The GENIUS Act, S. 394, remains pending** and has not become public law [*(Congress.gov, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/394)
- **The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act of 2025, H.R. 3633, remains pending** and has not become public law [*(Congress.gov, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/3633)
- **Executive Order 14233, "Establishment of the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and United States Digital Asset Stockpile,"** was signed on March 6, 2025, and published on March 11, 2025 [*(Federal Register, 2025)*](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/11/2025-03944/establishment-of-the-strategic-bitcoin-reserve-and-united-states-digital-asset-stockpile)
- **That order states the reserve will be capitalized with bitcoin already held by the U.S. government through criminal or civil asset forfeiture**, rather than by open-market purchases mandated in the order itself [*(Federal Register, 2025)*](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/11/2025-03944/establishment-of-the-strategic-bitcoin-reserve-and-united-states-digital-asset-stockpile)
- **The SEC approved spot bitcoin exchange-traded product listings in Release No. 34-99306** on January 10, 2024 [*(SEC, 2024)*](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nysearca/2024/34-99306.pdf)
- **The SEC continues to publish crypto-related enforcement and litigation releases** [*(SEC, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases)
- **The CFTC continues to publish digital-asset and digital-commodity enforcement actions** [*(CFTC, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.cftc.gov/LawRegulation/EnforcementCases/index.htm)
- **The Financial Stability Oversight Council's 2024 annual report said crypto-asset activities and stablecoins can pose risks to financial stability if interconnections and runs intensify** [*(FSOC, 2024)*](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/261/FSOC2024AnnualReport.pdf)

For readers, the key distinction is between access and legal certainty. Access to crypto exposure and tokenized financial products has widened, but stablecoin reserve rules, token classification, exchange obligations, and federal market-structure boundaries remain unsettled. These observations are not financial advice but provide a framework for discussion.

### Trade policy

Trade policy is still functioning as industrial policy, security policy, and an inflation variable at the same time. The central U.S. tariff actions were finalized in 2024, but their phase-in and supply-chain effects continue through 2026, while foreign retaliation and parallel measures abroad add to cost uncertainty.

- **USTR announced Section 301 tariff increases on May 14, 2024** on products including electric vehicles, batteries, semiconductors, solar cells, steel, aluminum, cranes, and certain medical goods [*(USTR, 2024)*](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2024/may/president-biden-takes-action-protect-american-workers-and-businesses-chinas-unfair-trade-practices)
- **USTR finalized the Section 301 modifications on September 13, 2024**, including **100% tariffs on Chinese EVs**, **50% on semiconductors**, and **25% on several battery, steel, aluminum, and other categories**, with some implementation extending into 2025 and 2026 [*(USTR, 2024)*](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2024/september/final-modifications-section-301-actions-chinas-targeting-maritime-logistics-and-shipbuilding-sectors)
- **The U.S. International Trade Commission reported that tariff pass-through can raise domestic prices and alter sourcing**, depending on market structure and supply elasticity [*(USITC, 2024)*](https://www.usitc.gov/research_and_analysis/trade_shifts_2024_special_topic.htm)
- **The European Commission imposed definitive countervailing duties on battery electric vehicle imports from China in October 2024** [*(European Commission, 2024)*](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_24_5589)
- **The IMF found that Red Sea shipping disruptions raised freight costs and delivery times**, showing how trade shocks can feed inflation even without a new tariff vote [*(IMF, 2024)*](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/03/28/red-sea-attacks-disrupt-global-trade)

The economic effect is broader than the targeted imports. Tariffs may support domestic investment in strategic sectors, but they can also raise intermediate-goods costs, slow goods disinflation, and push firms toward more expensive or less efficient sourcing.

### Fiscal policy

Fiscal policy is defined by a near-term financing constraint and a long-term debt trajectory that keeps worsening. The key issue for markets is not only annual deficits, but also whether debt-limit negotiations, Treasury issuance, and higher interest costs create spillovers into yields, liquidity, and private borrowing costs.

- **The Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023, H.R. 3746, Public Law 118-5, suspended the debt limit only through January 1, 2025** [*(Congress.gov, 2023)*](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-bill/3746)
- **Treasury states that the statutory debt limit was reinstated on January 2, 2025** [*(U.S. Treasury, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financial-markets-financial-institutions-and-fiscal-service/debt-limit)
- **CBO's Long-Term Budget Outlook projects debt held by the public rising from 100% of GDP in 2025 to 156% by 2055** [*(CBO, 2025)*](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61172)
- **CBO's Budget and Economic Outlook projects large continuing deficits over the 2026-2036 window**, keeping Treasury borrowing needs elevated [*(CBO, 2026)*](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61171)
- **Treasury continues to publish quarterly refunding statements and borrowing estimates**, which directly affect bill supply, coupon issuance, and market liquidity conditions [*(U.S. Treasury, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financing-the-government/quarterly-refunding)

For households and firms, this matters because larger deficits and higher net interest costs can keep pressure on Treasury yields, mortgage rates, and corporate financing costs even if inflation cools. Repeated debt-limit standoffs also create avoidable volatility in the market that anchors the global financial system.

### Monetary policy

The Federal Reserve remains restrictive, but March 2026 brought a meaningful balance-sheet adjustment. Rates were unchanged, yet the Fed slowed Treasury runoff sharply, which suggests greater caution about reserves, liquidity, and macro uncertainty even while inflation remains above the 2% target.

- **The FOMC kept the target range for the federal funds rate at 4.25%-4.50% on March 19, 2026** [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)
- **Beginning in April 2026, the FOMC lowered the monthly redemption cap on Treasury securities from $25 billion to $5 billion** [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)
- **The monthly cap on agency debt and agency mortgage-backed securities remained $35 billion** [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)
- **The December 2025 Summary of Economic Projections showed a median year-end 2026 federal funds rate of 3.9%** [*(Federal Reserve, 2025)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20251210.htm)
- **The March 2026 FOMC statement said uncertainty around the economic outlook has increased**, reinforcing a data-dependent rather than preset easing path [*(Federal Reserve, 2026)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)

The message is mixed but important. Monetary policy is still restrictive enough to restrain borrowing and risk-taking, but the slower pace of quantitative tightening reduces one source of pressure at the margin.

### International frameworks

Outside the United States, the biggest policy story is implementation. Europe has moved from passing AI and crypto rules to enforcing compliance calendars, while the United Kingdom continues a regulator-led model and China continues a targeted administrative-rule approach.

- **The EU AI Act entered into force on August 1, 2024** [*(European Commission, 2024)*](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)
- **The first EU AI Act prohibitions began applying from February 2, 2025** [*(European Commission, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)
- **The European Commission states that general-purpose AI obligations begin applying from August 2, 2025, and most high-risk system obligations from August 2, 2026** [*(European Commission, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)
- **Markets in Crypto-Assets, Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, established the EU-wide crypto framework** [*(EUR-Lex, 2023)*](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1114/oj)
- **ESMA states that MiCA Titles III and IV on asset-referenced tokens and e-money tokens applied from June 30, 2024, and the remaining provisions applied from December 30, 2024** [*(ESMA, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.esma.europa.eu/esmas-activities/markets-and-infrastructure/mica-regulation)
- **The UK government confirmed in February 2024 that it would pursue a pro-innovation, cross-sector AI regulatory approach rather than a single AI statute** [*(UK Government, 2024)*](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-regulation-a-pro-innovation-approach-government-response)
- **China's Interim Measures for the Management of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services took effect in 2023** under the Cyberspace Administration of China and other agencies [*(CAC, 2023)*](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm)
- **The OECD adopted updated AI Principles in 2024**, reinforcing international norms on trustworthy AI, risk management, and accountability [*(OECD, 2024)*](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0449)

The broader pattern is clearer rules, but not convergence. That can reduce uncertainty for firms able to absorb compliance costs, while increasing barriers for smaller entrants and for companies operating across multiple jurisdictions.

For economic preparation, the main conclusion is mixed rather than one-sided. Clearer rules can support investment, procurement, and product launches, but fragmented governance, tariff costs, debt-limit risk, persistent deficits, and still-restrictive monetary policy all raise planning complexity. That combination affects hiring, capital spending, payment infrastructure, and the speed at which AI and digital-asset systems move from pilot use into broader adoption.

## Financial System Evolution

Blockchain networks are increasingly being used as financial infrastructure for payments, settlement, collateral movement, and tokenized asset servicing, rather than only as venues for speculative trading. In 2026, the clearest signal is that regulated institutions, payment firms, custodians, and market infrastructures are building around stablecoins, tokenized funds, digital bonds, and interoperable data rails. This section explains how that plumbing is evolving; these observations are not financial advice but provide a framework for discussion.

### Cross-border payments and settlement

Cross-border finance remains one of the most practical testing grounds for blockchain infrastructure because legacy correspondent banking is often slow, expensive, and dependent on prefunded accounts. The structural shift is not that banks disappear, but that some messaging, treasury, and settlement functions are being rebuilt on faster, programmable rails.

- **RLUSD launched in December 2024** as a U.S. dollar stablecoin issued by Standard Custody & Trust Company, a firm supervised by the New York Department of Financial Services [*(Ripple, 2024)*](https://ripple.com/insights/ripple-usd-rlusd-is-live/) and [*(NYDFS, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/virtual_currency_businesses)
- **Ripple holds a Major Payments Institution licence in Singapore** through Ripple Markets APAC, placing part of its cross-border network strategy inside a regulated payments framework [*(MAS, 2023)*](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2023/mas-grants-major-payments-institution-licence-to-ripple-markets-apac-pte-ltd)
- **Ripple says its payments network covers 90+ payout markets**, highlighting that institutional adoption depends on payout reach and compliance coverage as much as on blockchain throughput [*(Ripple, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://ripple.com/payments/)
- **The Dubai Financial Services Authority approved RLUSD for use within the Dubai International Financial Centre in 2025**, adding another regulated jurisdiction to Ripple's stablecoin footprint [*(DFSA, 2025)*](https://www.dfsa.ae/news/dfsa-approves-ripples-rlusd-token-within-the-difc)
- **A U.S. federal court ruled in 2023 that Ripple's programmatic XRP sales on exchanges were not securities transactions**, reducing one major source of legal uncertainty around XRP's market treatment, even though other legal issues in the case continued [*(U.S. District Court, S.D.N.Y., 2023)*](https://www.nysd.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2023-07/SEC%20vs%20Ripple%207-13-2023.pdf)
- **MoneyGram Access uses Stellar** to support cash-in and cash-out for digital wallets, showing how blockchain rails can be paired with an established retail remittance network [*(MoneyGram, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.moneygram.com/mgo/us/en/stellar)
- **The BIS and Bank of England's Project Rosalind tested API functionality for a retail CBDC using multiple ledger environments, including Stellar**, illustrating Stellar's relevance to public-sector payment experimentation [*(BIS, 2023)*](https://www.bis.org/about/bisih/topics/cbdc/rosalind.htm)
- **The Stellar Development Foundation says the network has been used in aid disbursement and remittance-oriented pilots**, including work with UNHCR on blockchain-based cash assistance [*(UNHCR, 2022)*](https://www.unhcr.org/innovation/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/UNHCR-Stellar-Aid-Assist.pdf)

In practical terms, XRP and Stellar matter here because they are being positioned as settlement infrastructure inside regulated payment, treasury, and remittance workflows. The key educational takeaway is that blockchain adoption in payments is increasingly about reducing friction in back-end operations, not replacing the financial system wholesale.

### Real-world asset tokenization

Tokenization is moving fastest where the underlying asset is standardized, low-risk, and relatively easy to reconcile legally, especially short-duration government securities and money market exposure. That is why tokenized Treasury products have become one of the first institutional use cases to gain measurable scale.

- **Tokenized U.S. Treasuries were about $5.6 billion in market value on March 31, 2026** [*(RWA.xyz, 2026)*](https://app.rwa.xyz/treasuries)
- **The broader tokenized real-world asset market was about $19.7 billion on March 31, 2026, excluding stablecoins**, according to RWA.xyz tracking [*(RWA.xyz, 2026)*](https://app.rwa.xyz/)
- **Ondo says OUSG provides exposure to short-term U.S. Treasuries through BlackRock's BUIDL fund and related assets** [*(Ondo Finance, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://ondo.finance/ousg)
- **Ondo says USDY is backed by short-term U.S. Treasuries and bank demand deposits**, showing how tokenized yield-bearing instruments are being packaged for non-U.S. users [*(Ondo Finance, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://ondo.finance/usdy)
- **BlackRock launched the BlackRock USD Institutional Digital Liquidity Fund, BUIDL, in 2024**, giving the world's largest asset manager a tokenized cash-management product on blockchain rails [*(BlackRock, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.blackrock.com/cash/en-us/products/333396/blackrock-usd-institutional-digital-liquidity-fund)
- **Franklin Templeton operates the Franklin OnChain U.S. Government Money Fund**, one of the earliest registered U.S. funds using blockchain as a share registry and servicing layer [*(Franklin Templeton, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.franklintempleton.com/investments/options/mutual-funds/products/3314/oneriver/franklin-onchain-us-government-money-fund/benji)
- **DTCC acquired Securrency in 2023** to expand institutional tokenization infrastructure for compliant issuance and servicing [*(DTCC, 2023)*](https://www.dtcc.com/news/2023/october/19/dtcc-acquires-securrency-inc)
- **McKinsey estimated in 2024 that tokenized financial assets could reach about $2 trillion by 2030 in a base-case scenario, excluding cryptocurrencies and stablecoins** [*(McKinsey, 2024)*](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/from-ripples-to-waves-the-transformational-power-of-tokenizing-assets)
- **The BIS said in its 2025 Annual Economic Report that tokenization can improve transactions, collateral management, and recordkeeping if legal and operational foundations are strong** [*(BIS, 2025)*](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2025e3.htm)

The broader lesson is that tokenization is not starting with speculative assets. It is starting with cash-management products, government securities, fund shares, and collateral workflows, because those are easier for institutions, auditors, and regulators to supervise.

### Data oracles and interoperability

Tokenized assets only become useful at scale if they can connect to off-chain data, corporate actions, fund accounting, payment instructions, and bank systems. That makes data oracles and interoperability layers part of the financial plumbing, not just technical add-ons.

- **Swift reported successful experiments with Chainlink and multiple financial institutions to connect existing banking infrastructure with public and private blockchains for tokenized asset transfers** [*(Swift, 2023)*](https://www.swift.com/news-events/news/swift-unlocks-tokenised-value-international-financial-institutions-chainlink)
- **DTCC said its Smart NAV pilot with Chainlink and major asset managers demonstrated how mutual fund net asset value data could be delivered on-chain in a standardized way** [*(DTCC, 2024)*](https://www.dtcc.com/news/2024/april/03/dtcc-chainlink-and-leading-us-financial-institutions-complete-successful-pilot)
- **ANZ, Citi, BNY Mellon, and other institutions participated in Swift's tokenization experiments**, underscoring that interoperability work is being driven by incumbent financial firms rather than only crypto-native projects [*(Swift, 2023)*](https://www.swift.com/news-events/news/swift-unlocks-tokenised-value-international-financial-institutions-chainlink)
- **The Hedera Governing Council includes firms such as Google, IBM, Boeing, Deutsche Telekom, and Nomura**, which is central to Hedera's enterprise-governance model [*(Hedera, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://hedera.com/council)
- **The Coupon Bureau uses Hedera for AI-8112 universal digital coupon infrastructure**, a real-world example of a tokenized data network being used outside capital markets [*(Hedera, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://hedera.com/users/the-coupon-bureau)
- **Avery Dennison's atma.io uses Hedera for item-level supply-chain traceability and digital identity**, showing how distributed ledgers can support enterprise recordkeeping and provenance [*(Hedera, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://hedera.com/users/atma-io)
- **The BIS has argued that interoperability and unified ledger design are critical if tokenized money and tokenized assets are to settle together safely** [*(BIS, 2025)*](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2025e3.htm)

For readers trying to understand the system-level change, the important point is that tokenized finance depends on trusted data inputs and compatibility with existing institutions. Without reliable oracles, identity, and messaging standards, tokenized assets remain siloed pilots instead of usable market infrastructure.

### Stablecoin infrastructure and regulation

Stablecoins are becoming one of the clearest examples of digital assets functioning as payment infrastructure. Their importance comes less from ideology and more from utility: they can move dollar-like value continuously across borders, exchanges, wallets, and increasingly regulated payment environments.

- **The global stablecoin market capitalization was about $234 billion on March 31, 2026** [*(DeFiLlama, 2026)*](https://defillama.com/stablecoins)
- **The Financial Stability Board said in its 2025 progress report that authorities are strengthening regulation of crypto-asset activities and global stablecoin arrangements because of financial-stability and payment-system implications** [*(FSB, 2025)*](https://www.fsb.org/2025/10/crypto-asset-activities-progress-report-2025/)
- **Visa said in 2025 that it was expanding stablecoin settlement and cross-border applications with partners including Worldpay and Yellow Card** [*(Visa, 2025)*](https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.19881.html)
- **Stripe announced in 2024 that it would acquire Bridge to expand stablecoin-based global money movement infrastructure** [*(Stripe, 2024)*](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/stripe-to-acquire-bridge)
- **MiCA created the EU-wide framework for crypto-asset service providers, asset-referenced tokens, and e-money tokens** [*(EUR-Lex, 2023)*](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1114/oj)
- **ESMA states that MiCA's rules for asset-referenced tokens and e-money tokens applied from June 30, 2024, and the remaining provisions applied from December 30, 2024** [*(ESMA, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.esma.europa.eu/esmas-activities/markets-and-infrastructure/mica-regulation)
- **The European Banking Authority publishes supervisory and implementation materials for MiCA**, showing that the EU framework is now in an operational compliance phase rather than just a legislative phase [*(EBA, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.eba.europa.eu/regulation-and-policy/markets-crypto-assets-mica)
- **The GENIUS Act, S. 394, remains a bill in the 119th Congress and has not become law as of March 31, 2026** [*(Congress.gov, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/394)

The regulatory direction is becoming clearer even where legislation is incomplete. Stablecoins are increasingly being treated as payment instruments that require reserve backing, redemption rights, disclosures, compliance controls, and supervision of issuers and custodians. That is a major shift from the earlier period when much of the sector operated in a lighter and more fragmented policy environment.

### CBDCs and the public-private split

Central bank digital currencies remain an active area of policy experimentation, but the global picture is mixed rather than uniform. Many jurisdictions continue research and pilot work, while political resistance has grown in some countries, especially around privacy, surveillance, and the role of the state in retail payments.

- **134 countries and currency unions are exploring a CBDC as of March 2026**, according to the Atlantic Council tracker [*(Atlantic Council, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/cbdctracker/)
- **The Federal Reserve said in 2022 that it would not proceed with a U.S. CBDC without clear support from the executive branch and Congress, ideally through a specific authorizing law** [*(Federal Reserve, 2022)*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/money-and-payments-discussion-paper.htm)
- **The CBDC Anti-Surveillance State Act is pending in the 119th Congress and would restrict the Federal Reserve from issuing a CBDC directly to individuals** [*(Congress.gov, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1919)
- **The BIS Innovation Hub continues to focus heavily on wholesale CBDC and tokenized settlement experiments**, including cross-border and securities-settlement use cases [*(BIS, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.bis.org/about/bisih/topics/cbdc.htm)
- **Project mBridge reached a minimum viable product stage in 2024 before governance questions slowed broader rollout**, illustrating both the technical progress and geopolitical sensitivity of cross-border CBDC networks [*(BIS, 2024)*](https://www.bis.org/about/bisih/topics/cbdc/mcbdc_bridge.htm)

This creates a likely public-private split. In many markets, private stablecoins may handle customer-facing innovation in programmable payments and cross-border transfers, while central banks focus more on wholesale settlement assets, interoperability standards, and legal guardrails.

### Traditional finance integration

The strongest evidence that digital-asset protocols are becoming financial infrastructure is that incumbent institutions are integrating custody, tokenization, digital bond issuance, and institutional DeFi pilots into mainstream operations. This does not mean the transition is complete, but it does mean blockchain is increasingly being tested inside regulated financial workflows.

- **BNY launched a Digital Asset Custody platform for institutional clients in 2022**, giving large investors a regulated custody entry point [*(BNY, 2022)*](https://www.bny.com/corporate/global/en/newsroom/press-release/bny-mellon-launches-digital-asset-custody-platform.html)
- **State Street continues to position tokenization, fund servicing, and digital assets as part of its digital finance strategy** [*(State Street, accessed 2026-03-31)*](https://www.statestreet.com/us/en/individual/insights/state-street-digital-assets)
- **The Monetary Authority of Singapore said in 2024 that Project Guardian participants were advancing tokenized deposits, asset tokenization, and institutional DeFi pilots** [*(MAS, 2024)*](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2024/mas-announces-new-initiatives-under-project-guardian)
- **The European Investment Bank issued a €100 million digital bond on blockchain in 2024** [*(EIB, 2024)*](https://www.eib.org/en/press/all/2024-171-eib-issues-100-million-digital-bond-on-blockchain)
- **HSBC launched a digital assets custody service for institutional clients in 2024**, adding another major global bank to the custody buildout [*(HSBC, 2024)*](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2024/hsbc-launches-digital-assets-custody-service-for-institutional-clients)
- **The SEC approved spot bitcoin exchange-traded product listings in January 2024**, which matters here not as a price story, but as evidence that regulated brokerage, custody, and market-access infrastructure for digital assets has expanded [*(SEC, 2024)*](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nysearca/2024/34-99306.pdf)
- **The BIS argued in 2025 that tokenization and unified ledgers could reshape how central bank money, commercial bank money, and securities interact in settlement** [*(BIS, 2025)*](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2025e3.htm)

The key takeaway is that the financial system is adopting blockchain where it solves operational problems: faster cross-border transfers, programmable settlement, tokenized cash management, collateral mobility, and standardized data exchange. Risks remain substantial, including cybersecurity, legal enforceability, sanctions compliance, concentration in a small number of issuers and service providers, and fragmentation across chains and jurisdictions. Even so, the direction of travel is increasingly visible: digital-asset protocols are becoming part of the back-end plumbing of payments, settlement, custody, and asset servicing.

## Strategic Recommendations

The late March 2026 backdrop still favors preparation over prediction. Inflation has cooled materially from 2022 peaks, but many households remain squeezed by housing, insurance, interest costs, and thinner savings buffers, while labor demand is softening in some cyclical and routine-office categories rather than collapsing outright. These recommendations are not financial advice but provide a framework for discussion.

- **[CPI rose 2.8% year over year](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)** in February 2026, while **[core PCE rose 2.6% year over year](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-january-2026)** in January 2026 *(BLS, 2026; BEA, 2026)*

The practical implication is that the base case remains slower expansion rather than a confirmed recession, but the margin for error is small. Households are more exposed to overlapping pressures from essential-cost inflation, debt servicing, labor-market rotation, and asset-price volatility than they were during the low-rate period.

### Risk assessment

The biggest near-term threats to household financial stability are still inflation erosion in essentials, job displacement or slower hiring in AI-exposed and cyclical roles, a market correction after narrow equity leadership, and a geopolitical shock that lifts energy or goods costs. The risk is cumulative rather than singular, because several moderate stresses can arrive together.

- **[Shelter inflation was 4.3%](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.t02.htm)** year over year in February 2026 *(BLS, 2026)*
- **[Services less energy services rose 4.1%](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.t02.htm)** year over year in February 2026 *(BLS, 2026)*
- **[Temporary help services lost 12,300 jobs](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)** in February 2026, a useful early signal of softer hiring demand *(BLS, 2026)*
- **[The top 10 companies accounted for 37.3% of S&P 500 market capitalization](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/indexnews/announcements/20260112-1485232/1485232_sp500top10concentrationye2025.pdf)** at year-end 2025 *(S&P Dow Jones Indices, 2026)*
- **[The Federal Reserve's November 2025 Financial Stability Report highlighted elevated asset valuation pressures and the risk of sharp repricing](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-202511.pdf)** *(Federal Reserve, 2025)*
- **[Brent spot crude closed at $73.63 per barrel](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/hist/LeafHandler.ashx?n=PET&s=DCOILBRENTEU&f=D)** on March 24, 2026, while **[the IEA said non-OPEC+ supply growth is exceeding demand growth](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-march-2026)** in its March 2026 report, indicating calm prices but still fragile balances *(U.S. EIA, 2026; IEA, 2026)*

For discussion purposes, the most immediate household vulnerabilities are usually cash-flow fragility, high-interest debt, employment concentration, and overexposure to expensive assets. A family can face slower hiring, higher recurring bills, and weaker portfolio values at the same time.

### Diversification principles

Diversification is especially important when concentration risk is visible across markets, sectors, and income sources. Households that rely on one employer, one industry, one local housing market, or one investment theme are more exposed if growth slows or leadership rotates.

- **[The S&P 500 forward P/E was 21.4](https://www.yardeni.com/pub/peacockfeval.pdf)** in March 2026, above its **[5-year average of 19.9 and 10-year average of 18.3](https://www.yardeni.com/pub/peacockfeval.pdf)** *(Yardeni Research, 2026)*
- **[The S&P 500 closed at 5,776.65](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/indices/equity/sp-500/)** on March 24, 2026 *(S&P Dow Jones Indices, 2026)*
- **[The Nasdaq Composite closed at 18,182.62](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/index/comp/historical)** on March 24, 2026 *(Nasdaq, 2026)*
- **[The 30-year fixed mortgage rate averaged 6.67%](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)** for the week ending March 19, 2026 *(Freddie Mac, 2026)*
- **[Home prices rose 4.1% year over year](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/indexnews/announcements/20260224-1471000/1471000_cshomeprice-release-02242026.pdf)** in December 2025 *(S&P Dow Jones Indices, 2026)*

A practical diversification framework can include liquidity reserves, broad rather than narrow equity exposure, some inflation-sensitive holdings, and more than one source of earning power. The same principle applies to work: concentration in a single routine task set can be as risky as concentration in a single stock or sector.

### Inflation protection

Inflation protection in 2026 is less about one perfect hedge and more about matching tools to different risks. Positive cash yields help with near-term optionality, while inflation-linked bonds and selected real assets can help preserve purchasing power if services inflation or geopolitical commodity shocks reaccelerate.

- **[Series I savings bonds carry a 3.11% composite rate](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/savings-bonds/i-bonds/i-bonds-interest-rates/)** for bonds issued from November 2025 through April 2026 *(U.S. Treasury, 2025)*
- **[TIPS adjust principal with changes in the Consumer Price Index](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/tips/)** *(U.S. Treasury, accessed 2026-03-31)*
- **[FDIC insurance covers at least $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/)** *(FDIC, accessed 2026-03-31)*
- **[Gold moved above $3,000 per troy ounce](https://www.lbma.org.uk/prices-and-data/precious-metal-prices#/table)** in March 2026 *(LBMA, 2026)*
- **[Energy prices in CPI were down 0.2% year over year](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)** in February 2026, showing that commodities can hedge shocks but can also reverse when supply fears ease *(BLS, 2026)*

For discussion, households may think in layers: insured cash or high-yield savings for near-term stability, inflation-linked instruments for purchasing-power defense, and selected real assets or commodities for geopolitical shock sensitivity. Each option has tradeoffs in liquidity, taxes, volatility, and timing.

### Career and skills resilience

AI adoption is changing job tasks faster than it is eliminating entire sectors. The clearest labor-market pattern in early 2026 is resilience in health care and public-facing services, combined with slower hiring in some routine support functions and a growing premium on workers who can combine domain expertise with digital tools.

- **[Health care added 52,000 jobs](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)** in February 2026 *(BLS, 2026)*
- **[Government added 21,000 jobs, and social assistance added 11,000](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)** in February 2026 *(BLS, 2026)*
- **[41% of employers expect to reduce workforces where AI can automate tasks by 2030](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)** *(WEF, 2025)*
- **[39% of workers' core skills are expected to change by 2030](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)** *(WEF, 2025)*
- **[Workers in more AI-exposed occupations report greater task change and training needs](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/06/ai-jobs-and-skills_4e2d9e4b.html)** *(OECD, 2025)*

Sectors that currently look relatively resilient include health care delivery, skilled trades, energy systems, logistics, semiconductors, cybersecurity, compliance, defense-related manufacturing, and roles requiring trust, regulation, or physical presence. A durable preparation strategy is usually to build complementary skills, such as data literacy, AI tool use, communication, process improvement, and sector-specific expertise, rather than treating AI as a separate labor market disconnected from existing work.

### Geopolitical awareness

Geopolitical risk matters to households because it often shows up first in fuel, freight, prices, and market volatility rather than in a headline recession call. Oil prices, carry trades, sanctions, and tariffs all affect everyday financial conditions through inflation, borrowing costs, and employment in trade-sensitive sectors.

- **[The IMF found Red Sea attacks increased freight costs and delivery times](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/03/28/red-sea-attacks-disrupt-global-trade)**, with inflation effects arriving later *(IMF, 2024)*
- **[The Bank of Japan's policy rate remains around 0.50%](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2025/k250124a.pdf)** after its January 2025 increase, while **[the Federal Reserve remains at 4.25%-4.50%](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)**, keeping the yen carry trade relevant if rate gaps narrow further *(Bank of Japan, 2025; Federal Reserve, 2026)*
- **[The BIS warned that leveraged trades can amplify volatility when funding conditions shift abruptly](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2024e.htm)** *(BIS, 2024)*
- **[USTR finalized Section 301 tariff increases](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2024/september/final-modifications-section-301-actions-chinas-targeting-maritime-logistics-and-shipbuilding-sectors)** in September 2024, with some implementation extending into 2026 *(USTR, 2024)*
- **[The USITC reported that tariff pass-through can raise domestic prices and alter sourcing patterns](https://www.usitc.gov/research_and_analysis/trade_shifts_2024_special_topic.htm)** *(USITC, 2024)*

The practical use of geopolitical awareness is not constant trading or headline chasing. It is understanding that energy, shipping, and trade-policy disruptions can raise commuting costs, grocery bills, utility expenses, and financing costs, while also affecting local employers tied to manufacturing, logistics, exports, or imported inputs.

### Financial infrastructure literacy

Financial infrastructure is becoming more digital, more real-time, and more programmable, even for people who never directly hold a tokenized asset. Understanding that transition is useful as context for where payments, settlement, custody, and compliance may be heading, without treating the shift as a reason for speculation.

- **[Stablecoin market capitalization was about $234 billion](https://defillama.com/stablecoins)** on March 31, 2026 *(DeFiLlama, 2026)*
- **[The BIS said tokenization can improve transactions, collateral management, and recordkeeping if legal and operational foundations are robust](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2025e3.htm)** *(BIS, 2025)*
- **[The FSB said authorities continue strengthening regulation of crypto-asset activities and stablecoins because of financial-stability implications](https://www.fsb.org/2025/10/crypto-asset-activities-progress-report-2025/)** *(FSB, 2025)*
- **[The FedNow Service launched in 2023](https://www.frbservices.org/financial-services/fednow)** to support instant payments in the United States *(Federal Reserve Financial Services, accessed 2026-03-31)*
- **[MiCA is in force across the European Union](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1114/oj)**, giving a clearer legal framework for crypto-asset services and stablecoin-like instruments *(EUR-Lex, 2023)*

For readers, the key point is literacy rather than speculation. Over time, more financial activity may run on faster payment rails, tokenized fund structures, digital identity systems, and programmable settlement layers, which can affect how money moves and how institutions manage customer assets.

### Outlook across three horizons

A useful planning framework is to separate immediate resilience from medium-term flexibility and long-term adaptability. The next three years are likely to be shaped by slower but still positive growth, policy uncertainty, AI-driven task change, and continued modernization of financial infrastructure.

- **Next three months:** focus on liquidity, debt costs, and job resilience
  - **[The Fed is still holding rates at 4.25%-4.50%](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260319a.htm)** *(Federal Reserve, 2026)*
  - **[Mortgage rates remain near 6.67%](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)** *(Freddie Mac, 2026)*
  - **[Consumer sentiment was 73.6](https://data.sca.isr.umich.edu/)** in March 2026 *(University of Michigan, 2026)*
  - Discussion focus: emergency savings, variable-rate debt exposure, insurance deductibles, and contingency planning if hiring slows further

- **Next 12 months:** prepare for slower growth, sticky services inflation, and the possibility of a market correction after narrow leadership
  - **[Real GDP grew 2.3% annualized](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gross-domestic-product-fourth-quarter-and-year-2025-second-estimate)** in Q4 2025 *(BEA, 2026)*
  - **[The Leading Economic Index fell 0.2%](https://www.conference-board.org/topics/us-leading-indicators)** in January 2026 and was **[down 1.0% over the prior six months](https://www.conference-board.org/topics/us-leading-indicators)** *(Conference Board, 2026)*
  - **[The OECD projected global growth of 3.1% in 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-outlook-volume-2025-issue-2_4a60f2f4-en.html)** and warned that trade fragmentation and geopolitical tensions remain downside risks *(OECD, 2025)*
  - Discussion focus: balanced budgeting, diversified savings, refinancing awareness, and active career upskilling

- **Next 36 months:** expect AI adoption, industrial policy, fiscal strain, and payment-system modernization to keep reshaping labor demand and capital allocation
  - **[The IMF said medium-term U.S. growth will depend partly on productivity gains, labor supply, and fiscal choices](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/CR/Issues/2025/07/25/United-States-2025-Article-IV-Consultation-Press-Release-Staff-Report-and-Statement-by-the-575000)** *(IMF, 2025)*

  - Discussion focus: portable skills, manageable leverage, diversified assets, and financial literacy that keeps pace with digital infrastructure

The central message is cautious rather than alarmist. Households do not need to predict every rate move, market swing, or geopolitical event; they are generally better served by building liquidity, reducing fragile dependencies, diversifying exposure, and keeping skills current as AI and financial infrastructure continue to evolve.
