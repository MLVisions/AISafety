## Take Practical Steps

Preparation still matters, but the practical steps now need to reflect fast-moving risks and opportunities in 2025-2026. The guidance below focuses on actions individuals can take to reduce harm, improve adaptability, and make better decisions under uncertainty.

### Spot manipulation
**Use a verification routine, not just visual guesswork**

Deepfakes are getting harder to spot by eye alone. The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency advises people to **[verify suspicious content by checking the original source, cross-referencing trusted reporting, and treating urgent emotional content as a warning sign](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/recognizing-and-reporting-deepfake-and-synthetic-media)** *(CISA, 2025)*. The Federal Trade Commission also warns that **[AI voice cloning can imitate a family member or colleague in a scam call, so a callback to a known number remains one of the safest checks](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/scammers-use-ai-sound-just-family)** *(FTC, 2023)*.

Useful habits include pausing before sharing, checking whether the account is authentic, and looking for context mismatches such as recycled footage, altered captions, or audio that does not match the speaker's known cadence. For images and video, reverse-image search and provenance tools can help. The Content Authenticity Initiative explains that **[Content Credentials can attach verifiable metadata about how an image was created or edited](https://contentauthenticity.org/faq)** *(Content Authenticity Initiative, 2025)*, while the National Institute of Standards and Technology notes that **[synthetic media detection remains imperfect, especially when content is compressed, cropped, or re-recorded](https://www.nist.gov/itl/iad/mig/evaluating-deepfake-detection-tools)** *(NIST, 2024)*. In practice, the safest rule is to treat detection tools as aids, not proof.

### Verify & protect
**Layer identity checks and slow down financial decisions**

Scam prevention guidance has become more specific because impersonation fraud now uses AI-generated voice, text, and images. The FBI warns that **[criminals use generative AI to make phishing, social engineering, and financial fraud more believable at lower cost and larger scale](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA240409)** *(FBI IC3, 2024)*. The FTC advises consumers to **[avoid acting on unexpected requests for money, gift cards, crypto, or account credentials, and to contact the person or organization through a known, independent channel](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-imposter-scams)** *(FTC, 2025)*.

Practical protections work best when combined:
- **[Create a family safe word or verification question for urgent requests](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/scammers-use-ai-sound-just-family)** *(FTC, 2023)*.
- **[Enable multifactor authentication on email, banking, and primary social accounts](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/use-multi-factor-authentication)** *(CISA, 2025)*.
- **[Use a password manager and unique passwords for important accounts](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/use-strong-passwords)** *(CISA, 2025)*.
- **[Freeze credit files when fraud risk is elevated, which can make it harder for identity thieves to open new accounts](https://www.usa.gov/credit-freeze)** *(USA.gov, 2025)*.
- **[Report fraud quickly to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and, if internet-enabled, to the FBI IC3](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/)** *(FTC, 2025)*; **[https://www.ic3.gov/](https://www.ic3.gov/)** *(FBI IC3, 2025)*.

A useful personal rule is to move any high-stakes request into a slower channel. If a message creates panic or urgency, that is a reason to verify more carefully, not less.

### Reskill & adapt
**Focus on durable skills that work with AI systems**

Recent labor market research suggests that the most resilient workers are not only learning AI tools, but also building complementary skills. The World Economic Forum reports that **[analytical thinking, resilience and flexibility, leadership, creative thinking, motivation and self-awareness, technological literacy, empathy and active listening, curiosity and lifelong learning, talent management, and service orientation are among the fastest-growing core skills for 2025](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)** *(WEF, 2025)*. The same report says **[39% of workers' core skills are expected to change by 2030](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)** *(WEF, 2025)*, which makes continuous upskilling more important than one-time retraining.

For practical reskilling, prioritize combinations such as:
- technical literacy plus domain expertise, for example AI tools in healthcare, education, logistics, finance, or public service;
- data skills, including spreadsheets, statistics, SQL, data visualization, and evidence-based decision-making;
- workflow skills, such as prompt design, documentation, quality control, and human review of model outputs;
- interpersonal strengths that are hard to automate, including negotiation, teaching, caregiving, facilitation, and trust-building.

For accessible learning pathways, individuals can use public and nonprofit resources. The U.S. Department of Labor's CareerOneStop offers **[skill, training, and occupation exploration tools tied to local labor market data](https://www.careeronestop.org/)** *(U.S. Department of Labor, 2025)*. O*NET provides **[detailed task and skill profiles for occupations, which can help people identify adjacent roles rather than starting over](https://www.onetonline.org/)** *(U.S. Department of Labor, 2025)*. For those seeking formal credentials, community colleges and workforce boards remain important because the U.S. Department of Education notes that **[short-term credentials and career pathways can support transitions into in-demand fields](https://www.ed.gov/grants-and-programs/grants-higher-education/workforce-development)** *(U.S. Department of Education, 2025)*.

A practical rule is to build one skill in each of three buckets every six to 12 months: one AI tool skill, one domain skill, and one human skill. That mix reduces the risk of training for a tool that changes quickly.

### Build community
**Strengthen local trust networks before a crisis hits**

Community resilience matters because misinformation, fraud, job disruption, and stress often spread through social networks before institutions respond. The U.S. Surgeon General has warned that **[social connection is a critical public health factor, while loneliness and isolation are associated with higher risks of depression, anxiety, cardiovascular disease, dementia, and premature death](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf)** *(HHS, 2023)*. Strong local networks can therefore help with both information quality and mental wellbeing.

Effective community organizing usually starts small and stays practical. Helpful models include:
- hosting a local media-literacy or scam-awareness session through a library, school, union, faith group, or neighborhood association;
- creating a trusted channel for rapid verification, such as a moderated group chat with clear rules on sourcing and privacy;
- partnering with institutions people already use, especially libraries, which the American Library Association describes as **[key community hubs for digital literacy and civic information access](https://www.ala.org/tools/atoz/digital-literacy)** *(ALA, 2025)*;
- building mutual-aid capacity for job leads, childcare swaps, transportation, and emotional support during layoffs or fraud incidents.

For group effectiveness, public health guidance is useful: the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention emphasizes that **[community engagement works best when communication is transparent, culturally appropriate, and built on existing trusted relationships](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/communityengagement/index.html)** *(CDC/ATSDR, 2024)*. In practice, that means setting simple norms, sharing verified sources, protecting personal information, and avoiding public shaming when someone falls for manipulated content.

### Diversify investments
**Use diversification and liquidity as risk-management tools**

These recommendations are not financial advice but provide a framework for discussion. AI may reshape company earnings and labor markets, but concentration risk remains a major concern for households. The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission explains that **[asset allocation and diversification do not guarantee profit or prevent losses, but they are foundational ways to manage investment risk](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/asset-allocation)** *(SEC Investor.gov, 2025)*. The SEC also cautions that **[putting too much money into a single stock, sector, or theme can leave investors overexposed to one source of volatility](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/diversification)** *(SEC Investor.gov, 2025)*.

For individuals preparing for AI-related uncertainty, the most practical considerations are usually:
- keeping an emergency fund in a liquid, lower-risk account before taking additional market risk;
- checking whether retirement accounts are overly concentrated in a handful of large technology names through index funds, employer stock, or thematic funds;
- balancing long-term growth assets with cash or short-duration holdings if job security is uncertain;
- reviewing fees, tax consequences, and concentration risk before adding niche AI funds or crypto exposure.

Current rate conditions still make cash management relevant. The Federal Reserve states that **[monetary policy and interest rates affect returns on savings products, borrowing costs, and broader financial conditions](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy.htm)** *(Federal Reserve, 2025)*, so households should reassess cash, debt, and risk exposure when rates change. For retirement savers, broad diversification across domestic and international equities, bonds, and cash equivalents may be more resilient than trying to pick individual AI winners. If a person wants exposure to AI growth, it is generally safer to treat it as one part of a broader plan rather than the whole portfolio.
