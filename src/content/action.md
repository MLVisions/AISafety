## Take Practical Steps

Preparation still matters, but the most useful actions in 2026 combine fraud prevention, skill-building, social support, and basic financial risk management. Recent guidance from public agencies and labor market institutions shows that individuals are better protected when they use repeatable verification habits, build AI-complementary skills, and avoid overconcentration in any one platform, employer, or investment theme.

### Spot manipulation
**Use provenance checks, source verification, and context checks together**

Deepfake detection is still improving, but public agencies continue to warn that no single visual or audio cue is reliable on its own. The National Institute of Standards and Technology explains that **[deepfake detection tools can degrade when media is compressed, cropped, resized, or otherwise altered](https://www.nist.gov/itl/iad/mig/evaluating-deepfake-detection-tools)** *(NIST, 2024)*, and the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency advises people to **[verify suspicious synthetic media by checking the original source, comparing it with trusted reporting, and treating emotionally manipulative or urgent content as a warning sign](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/recognizing-and-reporting-deepfake-and-synthetic-media)** *(CISA, 2025)*.

Useful 2025-2026 habits include:
- **[Check whether an image or video includes Content Credentials, a provenance standard that can show how content was created or edited](https://contentcredentials.org/verify)** *(Content Credentials, 2025)*.
- **[Use reverse-image search and source tracing, because manipulated posts often recycle older or unrelated media](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/recognizing-and-reporting-deepfake-and-synthetic-media)** *(CISA, 2025)*.
- **[Treat AI voice calls with extra caution, because scammers can clone a voice from short audio samples](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/scammers-use-ai-sound-just-family)** *(FTC, 2023)*.
- **[Verify high-stakes claims through a known phone number, official website, or trusted newsroom before sharing](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/recognizing-and-reporting-deepfake-and-synthetic-media)** *(CISA, 2025)*.

A practical rule is to ask three questions before reacting: where did this first appear, who is independently confirming it, and what would happen if it were false? Detection tools can help, but **[NIST's evaluations emphasize that synthetic-media detection remains an active research problem rather than a foolproof consumer safeguard](https://www.nist.gov/itl/iad/mig/evaluating-deepfake-detection-tools)** *(NIST, 2024)*.

### Verify & protect
**Slow down urgent requests, harden accounts, and move verification off-platform**

Federal agencies continue to report that AI is making scams cheaper to run and more convincing. The FBI states that **[criminals are using generative AI to improve phishing, fraud, and social-engineering attacks at larger scale](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA240409)** *(FBI IC3, 2024)*, while the FTC warns that **[imposter scams often pressure people to send money, gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency before they have time to verify the request](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-imposter-scams)** *(FTC, 2025)*.

The most effective consumer protections are layered:
- **[Create a family safe word or verification question for urgent requests involving money or distress](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/scammers-use-ai-sound-just-family)** *(FTC, 2023)*.
- **[Enable multifactor authentication on email, financial accounts, and primary social accounts](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/use-multi-factor-authentication)** *(CISA, 2025)*.
- **[Use strong, unique passwords, ideally stored in a password manager](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/use-strong-passwords)** *(CISA, 2025)*.
- **[Freeze credit files when identity theft risk is elevated, because a credit freeze can restrict new credit from being opened in your name](https://www.usa.gov/credit-freeze)** *(USA.gov, 2025)*.
- **[Report fraud quickly through the FTC's reporting portal](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/)** *(FTC, 2025)* and **[the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center when the scam involved the internet](https://www.ic3.gov/)** *(FBI IC3, 2025)*.

A useful operating rule is to move any unexpected financial or identity-related request into a slower, independent channel. If a message creates panic, secrecy, or artificial urgency, **[FTC guidance says that is a core warning sign of an imposter scam](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-imposter-scams)** *(FTC, 2025)*.

### Reskill & adapt
**Build AI-adjacent skills that combine technical fluency with human judgment**

Recent labor market evidence suggests that workers benefit most when they pair AI tool familiarity with domain knowledge and durable interpersonal skills. The World Economic Forum reports that **[39% of workers' core skills are expected to change by 2030](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)** *(WEF, 2025)*, and identifies **[analytical thinking, resilience and flexibility, leadership, creative thinking, technological literacy, empathy and active listening, and lifelong learning among the fastest-growing core skills](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)** *(WEF, 2025)*. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics also projects continued demand in several AI-adjacent fields, including **[software developers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm)**, **[data scientists](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/data-scientists.htm)**, **[information security analysts](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/information-security-analysts.htm)**, and **[operations research analysts](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/operations-research-analysts.htm)** *(BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, accessed 2026)*.

Practical reskilling priorities for 2026 include:
- learning AI-assisted workflow skills, such as prompt design, output checking, documentation, and escalation when a model is unreliable;
- building data fluency, including spreadsheets, statistics, SQL, dashboards, and evidence-based decision-making;
- adding cybersecurity, privacy, or compliance basics, because AI adoption often raises governance and security needs;
- strengthening human-centered capabilities, such as teaching, facilitation, negotiation, caregiving, and client communication.

For affordable pathways, public tools remain strong starting points. **[CareerOneStop provides occupation, training, salary, and local labor market tools](https://www.careeronestop.org/)** *(U.S. Department of Labor, 2025)*, and **[O*NET offers detailed occupation profiles that can help workers identify adjacent roles and transferable skills](https://www.onetonline.org/)** *(U.S. Department of Labor, 2025)*. For shorter credentials, **[the U.S. Department of Education highlights workforce-development and career-pathway programs that support transitions into in-demand fields](https://www.ed.gov/grants-and-programs/grants-higher-education/workforce-development)** *(U.S. Department of Education, 2025)*.

A practical routine is to update one skill in each of three buckets every six to 12 months: one AI tool skill, one domain skill, and one human skill. That approach reduces the risk of training only for a tool that may change quickly.

### Build community
**Use trusted local institutions, clear norms, and repeatable communication channels**

Community resilience remains important because misinformation, fraud, layoffs, and stress often spread socially before formal support arrives. The U.S. Surgeon General's advisory states that **[social connection is a significant public health factor, while loneliness and isolation are associated with higher risks of depression, anxiety, cardiovascular disease, dementia, and premature death](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf)** *(HHS, 2023)*. Public health guidance also shows that **[community engagement works best when communication is transparent, culturally appropriate, and built on trusted relationships](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/communityengagement/index.html)** *(CDC/ATSDR, 2024)*.

Useful community practices include:
- hosting scam-awareness or media-literacy sessions through libraries, schools, unions, faith groups, or neighborhood associations;
- creating a moderated local channel for verification, with clear rules on privacy, sourcing, and rumor control;
- partnering with libraries, because **[the American Library Association identifies libraries as key community institutions for digital literacy and information access](https://www.ala.org/tools/atoz/digital-literacy)** *(ALA, 2025)*;
- organizing practical mutual aid, such as job leads, transportation help, childcare swaps, device help, and emotional support during layoffs or fraud incidents.

For group effectiveness, keep structures simple. Small groups tend to work better when they define who verifies information, how personal data is protected, when to escalate to authorities, and how to support people who have been targeted without shaming them.

### Diversify investments
**Prioritize liquidity, broad diversification, and concentration checks**

These recommendations are not financial advice but provide a framework for discussion. Households facing AI-related job or market uncertainty generally benefit from reviewing both portfolio concentration and cash resilience. The SEC explains that **[asset allocation and diversification are foundational risk-management tools, although they do not guarantee profits or prevent losses](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/asset-allocation)** *(SEC Investor.gov, 2025)*, and also warns that **[concentrating too much money in a single stock, sector, or investment theme can increase exposure to volatility](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/diversification)** *(SEC Investor.gov, 2025)*.

Practical considerations in 2026 include:
- keeping enough liquid savings for emergencies before adding more exposure to volatile assets;
- checking whether retirement accounts are unintentionally concentrated in large technology firms through index funds, employer stock, or thematic funds;
- reviewing debt costs and savings yields as interest-rate conditions change, because **[Federal Reserve policy influences borrowing costs, savings returns, and broader financial conditions](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy.htm)** *(Federal Reserve, 2025)*;
- treating niche AI funds or crypto exposure as higher-risk slices of a broader plan rather than a full strategy.

For many households, the key question is not whether AI will matter, but whether their finances are too dependent on one employer, one sector, or one market narrative. Broad diversification across asset classes, geographies, and time horizons may be more resilient than trying to pick individual AI winners, especially when employment risk and market enthusiasm can rise at the same time.
