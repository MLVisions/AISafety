AI & Technology

Capabilities, investment and the evolution of agentic AI

AI Capabilities Today

Recent frontier releases show that AI capability gains are no longer limited to chat. OpenAI announced GPT-4.5 as a research preview in February 2025 (OpenAI, 2025-02-27), Anthropic launched Claude 3.7 Sonnet with extended thinking in February 2025 (Anthropic, 2025-02-24), and Google introduced Gemini 2.5 Pro in March 2025 as a reasoning-focused multimodal model (Google, 2025-03-25). These releases build on the shift from 2023 systems that mainly excelled at text generation to 2024-2025 systems that can combine text, code, images, audio, and tool use in a single workflow. Historically, GPT-4 reached the 90th percentile on a simulated Uniform Bar Examination (OpenAI, 2023-03-15), and research published in PLOS Digital Health found ChatGPT performed at or near the passing threshold on the USMLE (PLOS Digital Health, 2023-02-09), but newer models are increasingly judged on coding, agentic software tasks, and multimodal interaction rather than exams alone.

Benchmarking has also become more practical. SWE-bench Verified was introduced to measure whether models can resolve real GitHub issues in actual codebases (OpenAI, 2024-08-13), reflecting a broader move away from static multiple-choice tests such as MMLU toward end-to-end task benchmarks. On coding benchmarks, Anthropic reported Claude 3.7 Sonnet achieved state-of-the-art performance on SWE-bench Verified at launch (Anthropic, 2025-02-24), while Google stated Gemini 2.5 Pro leads on several reasoning and coding evaluations, including GPQA and AIME 2025, in its launch materials (Google, 2025-03-25). For software generation, older coding tests still matter for historical context, and OpenAI's HumanEval benchmark remains a standard reference for code synthesis (OpenAI, 2021-07-07), but buyers increasingly care whether a model can edit repositories, run tools, and recover from failures.

Real-world deployment has broadened beyond demonstrations. In healthcare, the U.S. FDA has published a running list of authorized AI- and machine learning-enabled medical devices, which now numbers in the hundreds (U.S. FDA, accessed 2026-03-25), showing that image analysis, triage support, and diagnostic assistance are already in clinical workflows. In legal work, Thomson Reuters announced expanded generative AI capabilities in CoCounsel for legal and tax professionals (Thomson Reuters, 2024-04-02), and LexisNexis has rolled out Lexis+ AI for drafting, summarization, and legal research (LexisNexis, 2025-01-14). In software engineering, Microsoft reported that GitHub Copilot surpassed 1.3 million paid subscribers and was adopted by tens of thousands of organizations (Microsoft, 2024-04-25), indicating that coding copilots have moved from novelty to enterprise tooling.

Multimodal capability is now a defining feature of leading systems. OpenAI introduced GPT-4o in May 2024 as a model that accepts and generates text, audio, and images with near real-time voice interaction (OpenAI, 2024-05-13). Google's Gemini models support multimodal reasoning across text, image, audio, and video inputs (Google, 2023-12-06), and OpenAI launched Sora in December 2024 for video generation from text, image, and video prompts (OpenAI, 2024-12-09). This means frontier AI can now transcribe meetings, analyze charts and medical images, generate code from screenshots, create synthetic voices, and produce short videos, all within one product family.

The open-source versus closed-source landscape remains split. Closed models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google still dominate many headline evaluations and commercial deployments, but open-weight systems have improved rapidly. Meta released Llama 3 in April 2024 (Meta, 2024-04-18), and Meta released Llama 3.1 in July 2024, including a 405B-parameter open-weight model (Meta, 2024-07-23). Mistral has also published open-weight models and enterprise APIs (Mistral, 2024-02-26). In practice, this creates a two-track market: frontier closed models often lead on integrated multimodal products and proprietary tooling, while open-weight models give companies more control over cost, customization, privacy, and on-premises deployment.

Capability gains also sharpen misuse risks. The FBI warned in 2025 that criminals are using generative AI to make fraud and social engineering more scalable and more convincing (FBI IC3, 2025-03-18). The Federal Trade Commission has warned that AI voice cloning is enabling impersonation scams (FTC, 2023-03-20), and the World Economic Forum has documented how synthetic media can intensify election misinformation and identity fraud (WEF, 2024-01-10). As models become better at speech, video, and autonomous tool use, the same systems that help with customer service or coding can also be adapted into deepfake pipelines, voice phishing, and semi-automated scam agents. The central policy challenge is no longer whether these capabilities exist, but how quickly institutions can verify identity, authenticate media, and set guardrails around high-risk deployments.

Investment & Economic Impact

Private AI investment remains heavily concentrated in the United States. U.S. private AI investment reached $109.1 billion in 2024, while China attracted $9.3 billion and the U.K. $4.5 billion (Stanford AI Index, 2025, published April 2025). The same report shows that global private AI investment rose to $252.3 billion in 2024, up 25.5% year over year, indicating that capital is still flowing despite tighter financing conditions in the broader technology sector. For readers tracking competitiveness, this means the U.S. still dominates frontier-model financing, while China, the U.K., Israel, Canada, and France remain important secondary hubs by number of notable AI models and investment activity (Stanford AI Index, April 2025).

The infrastructure buildout is accelerating through hyperscaler spending. Alphabet expects approximately $75 billion of capital expenditures in 2025 (Alphabet, February 4, 2025), Microsoft expects capital expenditures to increase materially on a sequential basis in fiscal 2026 after spending $55.7 billion in fiscal 2025 (Microsoft, July 30, 2025), Meta raised its 2025 capital expenditure outlook to $64-72 billion (Meta, July 30, 2025), and Amazon guided to roughly $100 billion in 2025 capital expenditures, primarily for AWS and generative AI capacity (Amazon, February 6, 2025). At the industry level, global data center capital expenditure is projected to exceed $1 trillion annually by 2029, with generative AI a major driver (McKinsey, October 29, 2024). These recommendations are not financial advice, but provide a framework for discussion about how AI demand is reshaping semiconductors, power, networking, and cloud economics.

Adoption is broadening from experimentation to deployment. 78% of organizations reported using AI in at least one business function in 2024, up from 72% in early 2024 and 55% a year earlier (McKinsey, March 12, 2025). On the consumer side, OpenAI said ChatGPT had 400 million weekly active users as of February 2025 (OpenAI, February 20, 2025), showing that mass-market usage continues to expand even as enterprises focus on cost control and governance. In the workplace, 40% of workers in the European Union were exposed to AI in 2024, but only 13.5% reported using it on the job (European Commission JRC, February 20, 2025), suggesting that access is spreading faster than effective integration.

Long-run GDP projections remain large, but they vary by methodology and adoption speed. PwC estimates AI could contribute up to $15.7 trillion to the global economy in 2030 (PwC, accessed March 25, 2026; original study 2017, still widely cited). More recent institutional work is somewhat more conservative in annual-flow terms: McKinsey estimates generative AI could add $2.6 trillion to $4.4 trillion annually across 63 analyzed use cases (McKinsey, June 14, 2023), while the IMF estimates AI could raise global output by about 0.5% per year between 2025 and 2030 (IMF, October 2024). The IMF also warns that gains may be uneven because roughly 40% of global employment is exposed to AI, rising to 60% in advanced economies (IMF, January 14, 2024).

Labor-market forecasts continue to show both displacement and creation. The World Economic Forum projects 92 million jobs displaced and 170 million created globally by 2030, for a net gain of 78 million jobs (WEF, January 2025), updating its earlier 2023 estimate. The distribution matters: clerical and routine information-processing roles remain more exposed, while demand is rising for AI specialists, cybersecurity professionals, data analysts, teachers, and care workers according to the same report (WEF, 2025). The OECD finds a more nuanced near-term picture, reporting that workers in more AI-exposed occupations have not yet experienced systematically worse employment outcomes, but job quality and surveillance concerns are increasing (OECD, 2024). The ILO similarly concludes that generative AI is more likely to transform jobs than eliminate them outright, with clerical work facing the highest automation exposure (ILO, August 21, 2023).

Measured productivity results are now moving from theory to evidence. In one of the best-known field studies, customer-support agents using a generative AI assistant increased productivity by 14%, with the largest gains among less-experienced workers (Brynjolfsson, Li, and Raymond, Science, 2023). A randomized controlled trial at Procter & Gamble found that generative AI let individual professionals perform at a level comparable to teams and improved speed, quality, and cross-functional performance (Dell'Acqua et al., SSRN, 2024). At the macro level, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reported 2.7% labor productivity growth in 2024 (BLS, accessed March 25, 2026), but economists have not yet attributed most of that gain specifically to AI, which is a useful reminder that broad economic effects usually lag behind technical breakthroughs.

Taken together, the current picture is mixed but clearer than it was a year ago: capital spending is surging, enterprise adoption is rising, and credible studies show real productivity gains, yet labor-market disruption, uneven diffusion, energy demand, and concentration of compute remain significant constraints. For households, workers, and policymakers, the practical question is no longer whether AI will affect economic outcomes, but how quickly institutions can convert adoption into broad-based gains rather than narrow concentration.

Agentic AI & Swarm Architecture

Recent agentic AI development has shifted from open-ended autonomy toward controlled workflows with explicit tool use, state management, and human oversight. OpenAI introduced the Responses API and built-in tools for web search, file search, and computer use on March 11, 2025 (OpenAI, 2025), while Anthropic documented production patterns for tool use, extended thinking, and computer interaction in its developer guidance (Anthropic, accessed 2026-03-25). In practice, this means modern agents are increasingly designed as bounded systems that call models, retrieve context, execute approved actions, and log each step for review, rather than as fully autonomous digital workers.

Agent frameworks have matured quickly. LangGraph is presented by LangChain as a framework for building stateful, multi-actor applications with long-running workflows (LangChain, accessed 2026-03-25), Microsoft positions AutoGen as a framework for multi-agent AI applications that can use tools and humans in the loop (Microsoft, accessed 2026-03-25), and CrewAI describes its framework as role-based agent orchestration for collaborative task execution (CrewAI, accessed 2026-03-25). These frameworks differ in style, but they converge on a few common patterns: a planner or supervisor agent, specialist sub-agents, structured memory, tool wrappers, and evaluation or guardrail layers.

Real-world deployment has become more concrete, especially in customer support, software engineering, research assistance, and enterprise operations. Microsoft reported that its Copilot Studio can orchestrate autonomous agents for business processes such as sales lead qualification, supplier communications, and account management (Microsoft, 2024). Google Cloud states that Vertex AI Agent Builder supports enterprise agents grounded in company data and connected to business systems (Google Cloud, accessed 2026-03-25). Salesforce launched Agentforce as a platform for AI agents operating across CRM workflows with human handoff and policy controls (Salesforce, accessed 2026-03-25). The clearest near-term use cases are narrow and repetitive, where the system can fetch records, draft actions, request approval, and update systems of record.

Tool use is now central to agent capability. Anthropic's tool-use documentation explains how models can call external functions, use computer interfaces, and return structured outputs (Anthropic, accessed 2026-03-25). OpenAI's March 2025 agent tooling release explicitly added support for computer use and file search (OpenAI, 2025). Google DeepMind's Gemini API documentation supports function calling and code execution for external actions and computation (Google, accessed 2026-03-25). This is why current agent systems often outperform plain chat interfaces on real tasks: the model is not only generating text, it is selecting tools, manipulating files, browsing interfaces, and checking intermediate results.

Multi-agent orchestration remains an active research area, but the field is becoming more cautious about when swarms are actually useful. A 2024 survey on large language model-based multi-agent systems reviewed core architectures including supervisor-worker, debate, role-playing, and hierarchical collaboration (Wang et al., 2024). Another 2024 survey found that multi-agent systems can improve robustness and specialization, but also introduce coordination overhead, error propagation, and evaluation difficulty (Guo et al., 2024). A practical takeaway is that multi-agent designs are most valuable when tasks can be decomposed cleanly, such as research plus verification, planning plus execution, or coding plus testing. For many business workflows, a single orchestrated agent with tools is still simpler, cheaper, and easier to govern.

Safety and alignment challenges are more acute for agentic systems than for static chatbots because agents can take actions, persist state, and interact with external systems. Anthropic warns that agentic systems require safeguards for prompt injection, tool misuse, excessive autonomy, and data access control (Anthropic, accessed 2026-03-25). OpenAI's operator-style computer use research notes that browser and interface actions require confirmation and monitoring because mistakes can have real-world consequences (OpenAI, 2025). The UK AI Safety Institute highlighted in its agent evaluations research that increasingly capable agents raise concerns around oversight, control, and risky autonomous behavior (UK AI Safety Institute, accessed 2026-03-25). For readers building or buying these systems, the governance checklist is becoming clearer: restrict tools by default, require approvals for irreversible actions, log every step, test against prompt injection, and measure performance on complete workflows, not just model benchmarks.

Historically, the idea of autonomous AI agents has circulated for years, but the 2024-2026 shift is that agentic systems are now being productized through enterprise platforms and API primitives. The market is moving away from the earliest "auto-agent" demos that tried to do everything with minimal supervision, and toward observable, domain-specific systems that combine LLMs with retrieval, software tools, and policy controls. That makes agentic AI more useful, but it also makes implementation quality, permissions design, and human review far more important.

Future Trends & Opportunities

Near-term opportunity is shifting from general-purpose chatbots toward physical-world deployment, infrastructure bottlenecks, and more capable model architectures. In robotics, NVIDIA, Google DeepMind, and Disney announced Newton, an open-source physics engine for robotics built on NVIDIA Warp, with an initial release targeted for later in 2025 (NVIDIA, 2025-03-18), while Figure introduced Helix, a vision-language-action model for upper-body humanoid control, and reported it can run on embedded low-power GPUs for real-time behavior (Figure, 2025-02-20). In autonomous vehicles, commercial deployment is moving from pilots to scaled service footprints: Waymo said it was serving more than 200,000 paid passenger trips each week across its operating cities (Waymo, 2024-08-20, page updated after expansion announcements), and Uber and Waymo announced an expanded partnership to offer Waymo rides in Austin and Atlanta through the Uber app (Uber, 2024-09-13). The practical implication is that embodied AI is no longer only a long-term concept, it is becoming a near-term market for logistics, warehousing, industrial inspection, and geofenced ride-hailing, though broad household adoption remains much farther away.

Compute and semiconductor supply chains remain the clearest near-term constraint and opportunity. TSMC announced plans to invest an additional $100 billion in advanced semiconductor manufacturing in the United States, bringing its total planned U.S. investment to $165 billion (TSMC, 2025-03-03), and the U.S. Department of Commerce finalized up to $6.6 billion in direct funding for TSMC Arizona under the CHIPS and Science Act (U.S. Department of Commerce, 2024-11-15). On the demand side, hyperscalers continue to raise AI infrastructure spending: Alphabet said it expects approximately $75 billion of capital expenditures in 2025, primarily for technical infrastructure such as servers and data centers (Alphabet, 2025-02-04), while Microsoft said cloud and AI-related spending would continue to grow as it expands data center capacity (Microsoft, 2025-01-29). This creates openings not only for chip designers and cloud vendors, but also for power equipment, cooling, networking, memory, packaging, and grid modernization providers. It also means AI growth is increasingly tied to electricity availability, permitting timelines, and advanced packaging capacity, not just model quality.

Model development itself is moving beyond raw scale toward architectures that improve planning, tool use, and interaction with dynamic environments. OpenAI introduced o1 as a model series trained to spend more time reasoning before responding (OpenAI, 2024-09-12), and Google DeepMind described Genie 2 as a large-scale foundation world model capable of generating interactive 3D environments from prompts and images (Google DeepMind, 2024-12-04). Meanwhile, neurosymbolic and structured reasoning approaches continue to attract research attention because they aim to combine neural pattern recognition with explicit rules or search, which can improve verifiability in domains such as science, law, and enterprise workflows. For readers tracking longer-term potential, the key trend is that future systems are likely to blend reasoning models, world models, retrieval, and symbolic constraints rather than rely on a single monolithic architecture.

Government policy is becoming a direct market catalyst. In the United States, the CHIPS and Science Act authorized $52.7 billion for American semiconductor research, development, manufacturing, and workforce development (NIST, accessed 2026-03-25), and the funding pipeline has already supported multiple fabrication and packaging projects. In Europe, the European Commission said the AI Act entered into force on 1 August 2024, with obligations phased in over time (European Commission, accessed 2026-03-25), creating compliance costs but also opportunities in model governance, audit tooling, and safety engineering. In the United States, federal and state AI action plans, procurement programs, and defense-tech initiatives are also steering demand toward secure cloud, domestic manufacturing, and public-sector automation. For businesses, this means some of the most durable AI opportunities may come from regulated, infrastructure-heavy segments where policy support lowers adoption risk.

The main risk is not that AI progress stops, but that market expectations outrun deployment economics. The IMF reported that equity valuations in some AI-exposed sectors appear stretched relative to historical benchmarks, even though the macroeconomic payoff from AI remains uncertain and likely gradual (IMF, 2024-04-16). Public-market concentration is another warning sign: S&P Dow Jones Indices reported that the top 10 companies accounted for 37.3% of the S&P 500 at year-end 2024 (S&P Dow Jones Indices, 2025-01-02). When a small group of AI-linked firms drives index performance, any disappointment in revenue conversion, margin pressure from capex, export controls, or supply bottlenecks can trigger broader repricing. These observations are not financial advice but provide a framework for discussion: readers should watch whether AI revenue growth begins to justify infrastructure spending, whether enterprise adoption moves from pilot projects to budgeted workflows, and whether power, chips, and data center buildouts keep pace with demand.

Over the longer term, the most credible opportunities sit where AI is paired with scarce real-world assets or high-value decision workflows. Robotics could expand first in warehouses, manufacturing, eldercare support, and hazardous environments before consumer humanoids become mainstream. Autonomous driving is likely to spread city by city under tightly controlled operating domains rather than through sudden universal rollout. Semiconductor winners may include not only leading-edge fabs, but also firms exposed to advanced packaging, industrial automation, specialty materials, and energy systems. The broader lesson is that the next phase of AI may reward execution and infrastructure resilience more than headline model launches.
